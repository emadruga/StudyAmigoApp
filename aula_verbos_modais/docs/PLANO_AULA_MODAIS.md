## Seção 3 — Verbos Irregulares (Slides 4–6)

### Slide 4 — Irregular Verbs: What Are They?
- **Título:** Irregular Verbs
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Verbos regulares: **-ed / -d** para o passado e particípio
    - play → played → played
    - live → lived → lived
  - Verbos irregulares: **forma própria** — precisam ser memorizados
    - write → wrote → written
    - take → took → taken
  - As três formas: **V₁** (infinitive) | **V₂** (simple past) | **V₃** (past participle)
- **Visual:** Slide de corpo padrão; V₁/V₂/V₃ em negrito ou cor de destaque

---

### Slide 5 — Irregular Verbs: Table
- **Título:** Irregular Verbs — Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Tabela com verbos do cotidiano:

| Infinitive | Simple Present | Simple Past | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| to drive | drive(s) | drove | driven |
| to eat | eat(s) | ate | eaten |
| to buy | buy(s) | bought | bought |
| to run | run(s) | ran | run |
| to swim | swim(s) | swam | swum |
| to feel | feel(s) | felt | felt |
| to take | take(s) | took | taken |
| to go | go(es) | went | gone |

## Seção 4 — O que são Verbos Modais? (Slides 7–10)

### Slide 7 — Modal Verbs: Introduction
- **Título:** What Are Modal Verbs?
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Modal verbs give **special meaning** to the main verb
  - They express: ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice, deduction
  - The main modal verbs:
    - CAN / COULD
    - MAY / MIGHT
    - SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO
    - MUST
- **Visual:** Lista com as quatro famílias destacadas em cores diferentes

---

### Slide 8 — Modal Verbs: Grammar Rules
- **Título:** Modal Verbs — Grammar Rules
- **Conteúdo:** Três regras fundamentais:
  1. Always followed by the **base form** (infinitive without *to*)
     - She **can go**. (NOT: she can to go / she can goes)
  2. **No -s** in the 3rd person singular
     - He **must** leave. (NOT: he musts leave)
  3. **No auxiliary** needed for questions and negatives
     - **Can** you help? / You **must not** enter.
- **Visual:** Três caixas coloridas (azul/verde/laranja), estilo Woodward English; cada regra com exemplo correto e incorreto

---

### Slide 9 — Modal Verbs: Sentence Structure
- **Título:** How to Build Sentences with Modals
- **Conteúdo:**
  - **Affirmative:** Subject + modal + base verb + (complement)
    - She **should** drink more water.
  - **Negative:** Subject + modal + **not** + base verb
    - You **must not** eat before the blood test.
  - **Question:** Modal + subject + base verb + ?
    - **Can** you carry these bags for me?
  - **Perfect modal** (past reference): modal + **have** + past participle
    - He **could have taken** the wrong medicine.
- **Visual:** Quatro caixas, uma por estrutura; fórmula em destaque + exemplo abaixo

---

### Slide 10 — Modal Verbs: Overview Table
- **Título:** Modal Verbs at a Glance
- **Conteúdo:**

| Modal | Main Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| CAN | Ability (present) / Permission | I **can** swim very well. |
| COULD | Ability (past) / Polite request | I **could** ride a bike as a child. |
| MAY | Possibility / Permission (formal) | It **may** rain this afternoon. |
| MIGHT | Possibility (less certain) | I **might** go to the gym later. |
| SHALL | Suggestion / Offer (formal) | **Shall** I open the window? |
| SHOULD | Advice / Recommendation | You **should** see a doctor. |
| OUGHT TO | Moral obligation (formal) | You **ought to** call your parents more often. |
| MUST | Strong obligation / Deduction | You **must** take this medicine twice a day. |

- **Nota pedagógica:** Apresentar como visão geral; os slides seguintes aprofundam cada modal
- **Visual:** Cabeçalho em roxo escuro; destacar CAN/COULD/MAY/MIGHT (foco da Aula 1)

## Seção 5 — CAN / COULD (Slides 11–17)

### Slide 11 — CAN / COULD: Introduction
- **Título:** CAN and COULD
- **Conteúdo:**
  - **CAN** = habilidade ou possibilidade **no presente**
  - **COULD** = habilidade **no passado** / forma mais **polida** de CAN / possibilidade com menos certeza
  - COULD também é o **condicional** de CAN
- **Visual:** Dois cartões lado a lado (CAN | COULD) com fundo levemente colorido

---

### Slide 12 — CAN: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** CAN — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 15 do PDF, coluna esquerda):
  - **General ability (present):** I can speak English.
  - **Ask for permission (informal):** Can I borrow your pen please?
  - **To request something:** Can you help me please?
  - **Possibility:** It can get very cold there at night.
  - **Offer to help someone:** Can I carry your bags for you?
  - **Cannot (can't) = not allowed:** You cannot smoke in this room.

---

### Slide 13 — CAN: Everyday Context
- **Título:** CAN — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "I **can** wake up early on weekdays." / "**Can** I borrow your charger?"
  - *Saúde:* "**Can** I get this medicine without a prescription?" / "She **can't** eat gluten."
  - *Compras:* "**Can** I pay by card?" / "You **can** get two for the price of one today."
  - *Esportes:* "He **can** run 10 km without stopping." / "You **can't** use your hands in football."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio; ícones simples (sol, cruz, carrinho, bola)

---

### Slide 14 — COULD: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** COULD — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 15 do PDF, coluna direita):
  - **General ability in the past:** I could play the piano when I was younger.
  - **Ask for permission (more polite):** Could I use your bathroom please?
  - **To request something (more polite):** Could you pass me the salt please?
  - **Possibility in the past** (could + have + past participle): You could have broken your leg.
  - **Suggestion:** We could go to the movies if you like.
  - **Conditional of Can:** If we had some oranges, I could make you fresh juice.

---

### Slide 15 — COULD: Everyday Context
- **Título:** COULD — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "I **could** ride a bike when I was five." / "**Could** you turn off the lights, please?"
  - *Saúde:* "He **could have taken** the wrong pill — he wasn't paying attention." / "**Could** I schedule an appointment for tomorrow?"
  - *Compras:* "**Could** you show me a smaller size?" / "If they had it in stock, I **could** buy it today."
  - *Esportes:* "She **could** swim faster when she trained every day." / "We **could** win if everyone plays their best."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio

---

### Slide 16 — CAN vs. COULD: Side by Side
- **Título:** CAN vs. COULD — What's the Difference?
- **Conteúdo:**

| Situation | CAN | COULD |
|---|---|---|
| Ability (present) | I **can** cook pasta. | — |
| Ability (past) | — | I **could** cook pasta as a kid. |
| Permission (informal) | **Can** I leave early today? | — |
| Permission (polite) | — | **Could** I leave early today? |
| Possibility | It **can** happen anytime. | It **could** happen. *(less certain)* |
| Conditional | — | If I had flour, I **could** make bread. |

## Seção 6 — MAY / MIGHT (Slides 18–24)

### Slide 18 — MAY / MIGHT: Introduction
- **Título:** MAY and MIGHT
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Ambos expressam **possibilidade**
  - MAY ≈ 70% de chance | MIGHT ≈ 40% de chance
  - Para **permissão formal** e **desejos**, usa-se apenas MAY
  - MIGHT não tem forma de passado separada — usa-se *might have + past participle*
- **Visual:** Barra de probabilidade visual (0%–100%) com MAY em ~70% e MIGHT em ~40%

---

### Slide 19 — MAY: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** MAY — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 16 do PDF, coluna esquerda):
  - **Possibility:** It may rain later so take an umbrella.
  - **Give permission:** You may have another cookie if you like.
  - **Ask for permission:** May I borrow your pen please?
  - **Express wishes:** May the New Year bring you happiness.
  - **Speculate about past actions:** She may have missed her plane.

---

### Slide 20 — MAY: Everyday Context
- **Título:** MAY — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "I **may** be late tonight — traffic is bad." / "**May** I use your phone for a moment?"
  - *Saúde:* "The doctor said I **may** have an allergy." / "You **may** feel a little dizzy after this medication."
  - *Compras:* "This product **may** be out of stock by the weekend." / "**May** I try this on?"
  - *Esportes:* "It **may** rain during the match." / "She **may** not play today — she twisted her ankle."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio

---

### Slide 21 — MIGHT: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** MIGHT — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 16 do PDF, coluna direita — May or Might?):
  - **Possibility (less certain):** It might rain tomorrow. *(40% chance)*
  - MIGHT implies a **smaller chance** than MAY
  - For **wishes** and **permission**: only MAY is used
  - **Might + have + past participle** = speculation about the past
    - She might have missed her plane.

---

### Slide 22 — MIGHT: Everyday Context
- **Título:** MIGHT — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "I **might** go to the gym after class — I'm not sure yet." / "He **might** have forgotten to set his alarm."
  - *Saúde:* "You **might** need to see a specialist." / "She **might** have caught a cold at the game."
  - *Compras:* "The price **might** go up next week." / "I **might** buy that jacket if it goes on sale."
  - *Esportes:* "They **might** win the championship this year." / "He **might** not be fit enough to play on Saturday."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio

---

### Slide 23 — MAY vs. MIGHT: Side by Side
- **Título:** MAY vs. MIGHT — Key Differences
- **Conteúdo:**

| Situation | MAY | MIGHT |
|---|---|---|
| Possibility (higher) | It **may** rain. (~70%) | — |
| Possibility (lower) | — | It **might** rain. (~40%) |
| Give permission | You **may** sit down. | ✗ not used |
| Ask permission | **May** I come in? | ✗ not used |
| Express wishes | **May** you be happy! | ✗ not used |
| Past speculation | She **may** have left early. | She **might** have left early. |

- **Nota pedagógica:** Na prática cotidiana, MAY e MIGHT são frequentemente intercambiáveis para possibilidade — a distinção de % é didática, não rígida

### Slide 26 — Grammar Reminder: Perfect Modals
- **Título:** Modal + HAVE + Past Participle
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Used to speculate or comment on **past events**
  - Structure: modal + **have** + V₃
  - Examples:
    - She **could have taken** the wrong bus.
    - He **may have forgotten** his wallet at the supermarket.
    - I **might have left** my medicine at home.
  - The main verb is always the **past participle** (V₃)
- **Visual:** Fórmula em destaque + três exemplos com V₃ em cor diferente

---

### Slide 27 — Common Mistakes
- **Título:** Common Mistakes — Part 1
- **Conteúdo:**

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| She **cans** swim. | She **can** swim. | No -s in 3rd person |
| I **can to** drive. | I **can** drive. | No *to* after modal |
| **Can** she **swims**? | **Can** she **swim**? | Base form in questions |
| He **could** go yesterday. | He **could have gone** yesterday. | Past = modal + have + V₃ |
| **May** I **to** try this on? | **May** I try this on? | No *to* after modal |

## Seção 2 — SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO (Slides 4–12)

### Slide 4 — SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO: Introduction
- **Título:** SHALL, SHOULD and OUGHT TO
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Three related modals for **recommendation, moral obligation and suggestion**
  - **SHALL**: formal/dated; still used for offers and suggestions (especially in questions)
  - **SHOULD**: advice, recommendation, expectation — most common of the three
  - **OUGHT TO** ≈ SHOULD, but more formal; less frequent
- **Visual:** Três caixas lado a lado com as palavras em destaque

---

### Slide 5 — SHALL: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** SHALL — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 17 do PDF):
  - **Suggestions:** Shall I get a pizza for dinner tonight?
  - **Offers / Volunteering:** That bag looks heavy. Shall I carry it for you?
  - **Instructions:** What shall I do with your mail when it arrives?
  - **Promises:** You shall be the first person to know.
  - **Confirmation:** I shall meet you there at 7.
  - Nota: Shall pode ser substituído — Suggestion/Instructions → **Should** | Offers → **Can/Could** | Promises → **Will**

---

### Slide 6 — SHALL: Notes & Everyday Context
- **Título:** SHALL — Notes & Examples
- **Conteúdo:**
  - SHALL is **not common** in everyday modern English
  - Still found in **formal speech** and **written documents** (contracts, rules, menus)
  - In conversation, **Should** or **Will** is almost always preferred
  - Everyday examples:
    - "**Shall** we go to the park after lunch?" *(suggestion among friends)*
    - "**Shall** I carry your groceries?" *(offer at the supermarket)*
    - "**Shall** I book us a table?" *(making plans)*
- **Visual:** Caixa de aviso (borda laranja) para a nota de uso; exemplos em fundo neutro

---

### Slide 7 — SHOULD: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** SHOULD — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 18 do PDF):
  - **Advice or Suggestion:** You should get a haircut.
  - **Situation likely in the present:** Mary should be at home now.
  - **Likely in the future (prediction):** They should win tonight.
  - **Should + have + past participle:** You should have given your boss the report yesterday.
  - **Should + be + verb-ing:** You should be wearing your seatbelt.
  - **Shouldn't** = advise NOT to do something

---

### Slide 8 — SHOULD: Everyday Context
- **Título:** SHOULD — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "You **should** sleep at least 8 hours a night." / "You **shouldn't** skip breakfast."
  - *Saúde:* "You **should** see a doctor if the pain continues." / "She **should have taken** the medicine before eating."
  - *Compras:* "You **should** compare prices before buying." / "They **should** have more staff at the checkout."
  - *Esportes:* "He **should** train harder if he wants to make the team." / "You **shouldn't** run without warming up first."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio

---

### Slide 9 — OUGHT TO: Uses
- **Título:** OUGHT TO — Uses
- **Conteúdo:**
  - OUGHT TO can replace SHOULD without change in meaning
  - Sounds more **formal**; less frequent in speech
  - Structure: Subject + **ought to** + base verb
  - Examples:
    - You **ought to** drink more water. = You **should** drink more water.
    - You **ought to** visit your grandparents more often.
    - They **ought to have called** the doctor sooner.
  - **Negative:** ought **not** to (rare); in practice, *shouldn't* is preferred
- **Visual:** Caixa de comparação lado a lado — SHOULD ↔ OUGHT TO

---

### Slide 10 — SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO: Side by Side
- **Título:** SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO — Comparison
- **Conteúdo:**

| Modal | Register | Main Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| SHALL | Formal / dated | Offers, suggestions (questions) | **Shall** I carry your bags? |
| SHOULD | Neutral | Advice, expectation, prediction | You **should** eat more fruit. |
| OUGHT TO | Formal | Moral obligation, advice | You **ought to** call your doctor. |

- **Nota pedagógica:** Para ESL cotidiano, o foco prático é SHOULD — SHALL e OUGHT TO aparecem mais em leitura de textos formais e contratos

---

### Slide 11 — SHOULD: Common Mistakes
- **Título:** Common Mistakes — SHOULD
- **Conteúdo:**

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| You **should to** eat less sugar. | You **should** eat less sugar. | No *to* after modal |
| She **shoulds** rest. | She **should** rest. | No -s in 3rd person |
| I **should went** to the gym. | I **should have gone** to the gym. | Past = should + have + V₃ |
| You should have **go**. | You should have **gone**. | V₃ (past participle), not base form |

---

### Slide 12 — SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO: Practice
- **Título:** Practice — SHALL / SHOULD / OUGHT TO
- **Conteúdo:** Fill in the blanks:
  1. You _______ drink more water — it's really hot today. *(should / ought to)*
  2. _______ I carry those grocery bags for you? *(Shall)*
  3. The bus _______ arrive at 3:15 — it's usually on time. *(should)*
  4. She _______ have gone to the doctor sooner. *(should have / ought to have)*
  5. You _______ eat junk food every day — it's bad for your health. *(shouldn't)*
  6. Players _______ arrive at the stadium one hour before the game. *(should / ought to)*

## Seção 3 — MUST / HAVE TO (Slides 13–20)

### Slide 13 — MUST / HAVE TO: Introduction
- **Título:** MUST and HAVE TO
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Both express **obligation**, but from different sources:
    - **MUST** = obligation imposed by the **speaker** (internal/personal)
    - **HAVE TO** = obligation from an **external source** (rules, laws, third parties)
  - Doctor: "You **must** rest for three days." → the doctor is imposing
  - Patient: "I **have to** rest for three days." → external order from the doctor
- **Visual:** Dois balões de fala — médico (MUST) e paciente (HAVE TO)

---

### Slide 14 — MUST: Uses (Card)
- **Título:** MUST — Uses
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 19 do PDF):
  - **Obligation:** You must wear a seatbelt when you drive.
  - **Deduction (certain something is true):** Look at that snow. It must be cold outside.
  - **Emphasize Necessity:** Plants must have light and water to grow.
  - **Strong Recommendation:** We must get together for dinner soon.
  - **Mustn't = Prohibition:** You mustn't use your phone while driving.
  - Nota: **Had to** substitui MUST no passado — "I had to pay the speeding ticket."

---

### Slide 15 — MUST: Everyday Context
- **Título:** MUST — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "I **must** wake up at 6 a.m. tomorrow — I have an early class." / "You **must** try this recipe!"
  - *Saúde:* "You **must** take this medicine twice a day." / "She **must** be exhausted — she worked all night."
  - *Compras:* "You **must** keep your receipt to get a refund." / "This bread **must** be fresh — it smells amazing."
  - *Esportes:* "Players **must** wear shin guards during the match." / "He trains every day — he **must** really love football."
- **Visual:** Quatro mini-caixas coloridas, uma por domínio

---

### Slide 16 — MUST vs. HAVE TO
- **Título:** MUST vs. HAVE TO — Key Difference
- **Conteúdo:**
  - **MUST** → the speaker feels or imposes the obligation
    - I **must** call my mother. *(I personally feel I should)*
    - You **must** try this dish! *(strong recommendation from me)*
  - **HAVE TO** → the obligation comes from outside
    - I **have to** be at work by 8 a.m. *(company rule)*
    - We **have to** show ID to enter. *(external requirement)*
  - In **questions**, *have to* is more natural:
    - "Do you **have to** work on Saturdays?" *(NOT: "Must you work on Saturdays?")*
- **Visual:** Dois cartões coloridos — MUST (laranja/interno) vs. HAVE TO (azul/externo)

---

### Slide 17 — MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO (Card)
- **Título:** MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO
- **Visual:** Reproduzir o card Woodward English (slide 20 do PDF):
  - **MUSTN'T** = prohibited; it is NOT allowed
    - You **must not** drink that. = It is forbidden.
    - You **mustn't** tell John. = You are not allowed to.
  - **DON'T HAVE TO** = no obligation; you can do it if you want
    - You **don't have to** drink that. = You can if you want, but it's not required.
    - You **don't have to** tell John. = It's optional.
- **Nota pedagógica:** Este é um dos erros mais comuns de alunos de ESL — dedicar tempo à distinção

---

### Slide 18 — MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO: Everyday Context
- **Título:** MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO — Everyday Examples
- **Conteúdo:** Exemplos por domínio:
  - *Rotina:* "You **mustn't** be late for the exam." / "You **don't have to** wear a uniform on weekends."
  - *Saúde:* "You **mustn't** drink alcohol while taking this medication." / "You **don't have to** fast before this test — only before the blood work."
  - *Compras:* "You **mustn't** open the package before paying." / "You **don't have to** bring a bag — we have free ones here."
  - *Esportes:* "Players **mustn't** argue with the referee." / "You **don't have to** join the extra practice — it's optional."
- **Visual:** Duas colunas coloridas (vermelho/laranja para MUSTN'T | verde para DON'T HAVE TO)

---

### Slide 19 — MUST: Common Mistakes
- **Título:** Common Mistakes — MUST
- **Conteúdo:**

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| She **musts** rest. | She **must** rest. | No -s in 3rd person |
| I **must to** leave. | I **must** leave. | No *to* after modal |
| He **must** go yesterday. | He **had to** go yesterday. | Past of MUST = HAD TO |
| You **mustn't** bring ID. *(meaning: optional)* | You **don't have to** bring ID. | MUSTN'T ≠ DON'T HAVE TO |

---

### Slide 20 — MUST / HAVE TO: Practice
- **Título:** Practice — MUST / HAVE TO / MUSTN'T / DON'T HAVE TO
- **Conteúdo:** Fill in the blanks:
  1. You _______ run on the pool deck — it's slippery and dangerous. *(mustn't)*
  2. You _______ bring a gift to the party — it's not expected. *(don't have to)*
  3. All passengers _______ show their ticket before boarding. *(have to / must)*
  4. Look at that dark sky. It _______ be about to rain. *(must)*
  5. She _______ take an extra class last semester — it wasn't required. *(didn't have to)*
  6. I _______ leave early yesterday because my daughter was sick. *(had to)*

## Seção 4 — Modais na Voz Passiva (Slides 21–25)

### Slide 21 — Modal Verbs in the Passive Voice: Structure
- **Título:** Modal Verbs in the Passive Voice
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Structure: **Subject + modal + be + past participle**
  - The agent (who does the action) is often omitted or introduced with *by*
  - Examples:
    - This form **must be filled in** before your appointment.
    - The match **might be cancelled** due to rain.
    - Fresh fruit **should be kept** in the refrigerator.
    - The medicine **could have been** taken incorrectly.
- **Visual:** Caixa destacada com a fórmula; setas coloridas apontando para cada parte

---

### Slide 22 — Passive Voice: Why Use It?
- **Título:** Why Use the Passive Voice with Modals?
- **Conteúdo:**
  - To focus on the **action or object**, not who performs it
  - Common in **instructions, rules, signs, and labels**:
    - "This medicine **must be taken** with food." *(label — bula)*
    - "Trolleys **should be returned** after use." *(supermarket sign)*
    - "Pets **cannot be brought** into the store." *(shop rule)*
  - The agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant

---

### Slide 23 — Passive Voice: Examples Table
- **Título:** Passive Voice with Modals — Examples
- **Conteúdo:**

| Modal | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| must | You must refrigerate this product. | This product **must be refrigerated**. |
| should | They should wash the vegetables. | The vegetables **should be washed**. |
| can | Anyone can use this entrance. | This entrance **can be used** by anyone. |
| could | They could have cancelled the game. | The game **could have been cancelled**. |
| may | Someone may have taken the wrong bag. | The wrong bag **may have been taken**. |
| might | A customer might have left it here. | It **might have been left** by a customer. |

---

### Slide 24 — Passive Voice: Common Mistakes
- **Título:** Common Mistakes — Passive with Modals
- **Conteúdo:**

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| It must **be refrigerate**. | It must **be refrigerated**. | Past participle (V₃), not base form |
| It should **been** washed. | It should **be** washed. | modal + **be** + V₃ |
| It **could been** taken. | It **could have been** taken. | Past passive = modal + have + been + V₃ |

---

### Slide 25 — Passive Voice with Modals: Practice
- **Título:** Practice — Passive Voice with Modals
- **Conteúdo:** Rewrite in the passive voice:
  1. You **must sign** this form. → This form **must be signed**.
  2. Someone **might have moved** my bag. → My bag _______.
  3. They **should serve** the soup hot. → The soup _______.
  4. Customers **can return** the product within 30 days. → The product _______.
  5. A player **could have broken** the record. → The record _______.

## Seção 5 — Revisão Geral (Slides 26–30)

### Slide 26 — Summary: All 8 Modal Verbs
- **Título:** Modal Verbs — Full Summary
- **Conteúdo:**

| Modal | Function | Certainty / Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAN | Ability (present) / Permission | Neutral | I **can** swim. |
| COULD | Ability (past) / Polite / Conditional | Low–Medium | I **could** cook as a child. |
| MAY | Possibility / Permission (formal) | ~70% | It **may** rain later. |
| MIGHT | Possibility (less certain) | ~40% | I **might** go to the gym. |
| SHALL | Offer / Suggestion (formal) | Neutral | **Shall** we go? |
| SHOULD | Advice / Expectation | Medium | You **should** see a doctor. |
| OUGHT TO | Moral obligation / Advice (formal) | Medium | You **ought to** call her. |
| MUST | Strong obligation / Deduction | High | You **must** rest today. |

---

### Slide 27 — Key Distinctions to Remember
- **Título:** Don't Confuse These!
- **Conteúdo:** Quatro pares críticos com exemplos do cotidiano:
  - **MUSTN'T** (prohibited) ≠ **DON'T HAVE TO** (not required)
    - "You **mustn't** smoke here." vs. "You **don't have to** buy anything."
  - **MUST** (speaker's obligation) ≠ **HAVE TO** (external obligation)
    - "You **must** try this cake!" vs. "I **have to** be at work at 8."
  - **MAY** (~70% chance) ≠ **MIGHT** (~40% chance)
    - "It **may** rain." vs. "It **might** rain."
  - **CAN** (present ability) ≠ **COULD** (past ability / polite / conditional)
    - "I **can** run 5 km." vs. "I **could** run faster last year."

### Slide 28 — Final Practice: Mixed Modals
- **Título:** Final Practice — All Modals
- **Conteúdo:** Choose the correct modal:
  1. You _______ eat in the changing room. It's not allowed. *(mustn't)*
  2. She _______ play the guitar when she was ten. *(could)*
  3. _______ I try this jacket on, please? *(May / Can / Could)*
  4. The supermarket _______ be closed — it's a holiday today. *(may / might / could)*
  5. You _______ bring your gym bag; they provide towels there. *(don't have to)*
  6. All visitors _______ sign in at the reception desk. *(must / have to)*
  7. He _______ have eaten something bad — he feels terrible. *(must / may / might)*
  8. Medicines _______ be kept out of reach of children. *(must be / should be)*

### Slide 29 — Discussion & Production Activity
- **Título:** Let's Talk — Final Activity
- **Conteúdo:** In pairs or small groups, discuss:
  1. What **must** every person do to stay healthy? What **don't** they **have to** do?
  2. What **should** a good teammate do? What **shouldn't** they do?

### Slide 30 — Wrap-Up: Both Lessons
- **Título:** Key Takeaways — Modal Verbs
- **Conteúdo:**
  - Modal verbs **never change form** — no -s, no -ed, no *to* after them
  - modal + base verb → present / future meaning
  - modal + **have** + V₃ → past reference
  - modal + **be** + V₃ → passive voice
  - modal + **have been** + V₃ → past passive
  - Know your pairs: MUST/HAVE TO | MUSTN'T/DON'T HAVE TO | MAY/MIGHT | CAN/COULD
