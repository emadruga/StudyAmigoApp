## 4. Textos por tier (Component B)

> Os textos de E05 foram criados especificamente para este exercício, incorporando verbos modais nos 4 domínios cotidianos trabalhados em aula: rotina diária, saúde & farmácia, compras & supermercado, esportes & lazer.

### Tier 1 — Foundation

**Texto**: *"What You Can and Can't Do"*
**Tamanho**: ~175 palavras
**Foco gramatical**: CAN, CAN'T, MUST, SHOULD, COULD, MAY — modais básicos em frases curtas
**Contexto**: Um estudante brasileiro descreve regras, habilidades e possibilidades do seu dia a dia

---

#### What You Can and Can't Do

My name is Lucas. I am a student in Brazil. Every day, I follow rules and make choices. Some things I can do, and some things I can't.

At school, I must arrive before 7:30 a.m. I can't use my phone during class. If I feel sick, I should tell the teacher. She may let me go to the nurse's office.

At the pharmacy, I can buy some medicines without a prescription. But I can't buy antibiotics without one. The pharmacist can help me choose the right medicine. I should always read the instructions on the box.

At the supermarket, I can pay with cash or card. I must keep the receipt if I want to return a product. I should compare prices before I buy.

On weekends, I can play football with my friends. I could also go to the gym, but I must warm up first. We can't play without shin guards — it's a safety rule.

I should study more, but I could also rest a little. You can't do everything in one day!

*(175 palavras)*

---

**Lista curada (10 palavras)**:

As palavras-alvo são os **próprios verbos modais**. O aluno cria cartões cloze com `[CAN]`, `[MUST]`, etc.

| # | Palavra | Função / Significado | Contexto no texto |
|---|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **can** | habilidade / possibilidade (presente) | *I can play football with my friends* |
| 2 | **can't** | proibição / impossibilidade | *I can't use my phone during class* |
| 3 | **must** | obrigação forte | *I must arrive before 7:30 a.m.* |
| 4 | **should** | conselho / recomendação | *I should compare prices before I buy* |
| 5 | **could** | possibilidade / alternativa | *I could also go to the gym* |
| 6 | **may** | permissão (formal) / possibilidade | *She may let me go to the nurse's office* |
| 7 | **can't (proibição)** | regra externa | *We can't play without shin guards* |
| 8 | **must (obrigação)** | necessidade | *I must warm up first* |
| 9 | **should (conselho)** | recomendação | *I should always read the instructions* |
| 10 | **could (alternativa)** | outra possibilidade | *I could also rest a little* |

> **Nota pedagógica**: os itens 7–10 repetem modais dos itens 1–6, mas em **contextos e funções diferentes**. Isso reforça que um mesmo modal pode ter múltiplos usos (ex.: CAN'T = impossibilidade vs. CAN'T = proibição).

**Formato de cartão**: cloze simples — frente com `[MODAL]`, verso com `modal = função + tradução`
**Estrutura**: Nível 1 (substituição do modal na frase)

### Tier 2 — Developing

**Texto**: *"Rules, Advice, and Possibilities"*
**Tamanho**: ~300 palavras
**Foco gramatical**: todos os 8 modais (CAN, COULD, MAY, MIGHT, SHALL, SHOULD, OUGHT TO, MUST) + HAVE TO, MUSTN'T, DON'T HAVE TO
**Contexto**: Situações variadas nos 4 domínios cotidianos com uso natural dos modais

---

#### Rules, Advice, and Possibilities

Understanding when to use modal verbs is essential for communicating clearly in English. Modal verbs express ability, permission, obligation, advice, and possibility. Let's look at how they appear in everyday situations.

**Ability and Permission**

In sports, athletes can train every day, but they must follow the coach's instructions. A player who is injured could still attend practice to watch, but he can't participate. At the gym, you may use any machine after signing a waiver. If you are unsure about the equipment, you could ask a trainer for help.

**Obligation and Advice**

At the pharmacy, customers must present a valid prescription for controlled medicines. You should always check the expiration date before buying any product. Patients ought to follow the dosage instructions carefully. You don't have to buy the most expensive brand — generic medicines can be just as effective.

**Possibility and Speculation**

The weather might change this afternoon, so you should bring an umbrella. The supermarket may close early on holidays — it's best to check online first. That product could be cheaper at another store. You may have noticed that prices have gone up recently.

**Prohibition vs. No Obligation**

Players mustn't argue with the referee during a match — they could receive a yellow card. However, they don't have to shake hands after the game; it's polite but not required. At the supermarket, you mustn't open packages before paying. But you don't have to bring your own bag — the store provides them for free.

**Suggestions and Offers**

Shall we go to the pharmacy before it closes? You should try the new sports center — the facilities are excellent. We could go grocery shopping together if you like. If you need advice, you ought to ask the pharmacist — she might know which product is best for your symptoms.

**Past Speculation**

I can't find my wallet. I may have left it at the checkout. She could have forgotten to take her medicine this morning. The coach should have stopped the training earlier — the player might have avoided the injury.

*(~300 palavras)*

---

### Tier 3 — Expanding (sob demanda)

**Texto**: *"Safety Rules and Responsibilities: What Every Citizen Should Know"*
**Tamanho**: ~490 palavras
**Foco gramatical**: modais em registro formal, voz passiva com modais, perfect modals (modal + have + V₃), distinções MUST vs. HAVE TO, MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO
**Contexto**: Texto editorial sobre regras, responsabilidades e direitos do cidadão

---

#### Safety Rules and Responsibilities: What Every Citizen Should Know

In any organized society, rules exist to protect the well-being of its members. Understanding these rules — and the language used to express them — is essential for anyone living, studying, or working abroad. Modal verbs play a central role in how obligations, permissions, and prohibitions are communicated in English.

**Obligation and Necessity**

Citizens must obey traffic laws at all times. Drivers must not use their phones while driving, and seat belts must be worn by all passengers. These are legal requirements — violations can result in fines or even imprisonment. In contrast, wearing a helmet while cycling is strongly recommended but may not be required by law in every jurisdiction. Employers must provide a safe working environment for their staff. Hazardous materials must be stored according to strict regulations, and safety procedures must be followed without exception.

**Permission and Prohibition**

In most countries, medications that could be harmful if misused must not be sold without a prescription. Antibiotics, for instance, may only be dispensed by a licensed pharmacist. Customers may not return food products once purchased, although non-perishable items can usually be exchanged within a specified period.

**Advice, Recommendation, and Moral Duty**

Health authorities recommend that individuals should get vaccinated against common diseases. Parents ought to ensure their children receive regular medical check-ups. Students should manage their time effectively and ought to seek help when they encounter academic difficulties.

**Possibility, Deduction, and Speculation**

A person who has been exposed to an infectious disease may develop symptoms within days. Scientists believe that climate change could lead to more frequent extreme weather events. The government's new policy might reduce unemployment, but it could also increase inflation.

**Past Reference: Perfect Modals**

The accident could have been prevented if proper safety measures had been in place. The patient should have been informed about the side effects before starting the treatment. Investigators concluded that the fire must have started in the basement. The package may have been delivered to the wrong address — the tracking information should be checked.

**Passive Voice with Modals**

In formal and institutional English, modal verbs are frequently combined with the passive voice. Rules must be respected. Complaints should be submitted in writing. Dangerous goods must not be transported without proper authorization. The form must be filled in before the deadline. These structures are particularly common in regulations, labels, and official documents — exactly the kind of text that advanced learners must be able to read and produce confidently.

**Key Distinctions**

Understanding the difference between similar modal expressions is crucial. "You mustn't park here" means it is forbidden, while "You don't have to park here" simply means it is not required. Similarly, "You must see a doctor" expresses the speaker's strong personal recommendation, whereas "You have to see a doctor" suggests an external requirement — perhaps an employer's policy or a legal obligation. These distinctions may seem subtle, but they can change the meaning of a sentence and should not be overlooked.

*(~490 palavras)*

---

**Seleção de palavras**: autônoma — o aluno aplica auditoria produtiva com critério fino sobre os modais em contexto formal:
- Colocações formais: *must be worn*, *may not be permitted*, *could have been prevented*
- Distinções: MUST vs. HAVE TO, MUSTN'T vs. DON'T HAVE TO, MAY vs. MIGHT
- Modais na voz passiva: *must be stored*, *should be submitted*, *could have been prevented*
- Perfect modals: *should have been informed*, *must have started*, *may have been delivered*

**Formato de cartão**: definição em inglês — verso com `modal/expressão (classe) = definição EN` + `☞ colocações` + tradução da frase

## 5. Component C — Letras de Músicas e Transcrição de Vídeos

### Conceito

O Component C de E05 segue o mesmo modelo de E04: o aluno cria cartões de vocabulário a partir de **letras de músicas em inglês** e/ou **transcrições de vídeos educacionais do YouTube**.

### Fontes aceitas

| Tipo de fonte | Exemplos | Requisitos |
|---------------|----------|------------|
| **Letra de música** | Música em inglês (qualquer gênero) | URL da letra (Genius, AZLyrics, Vagalume, etc.) ou nome da música + artista |
| **Transcrição de vídeo** | Vídeo educacional no YouTube com legendas em inglês | URL do vídeo do YouTube |

### Rastreabilidade: Cartão-metadado

**Regra**: Para cada fonte utilizada, o aluno cria **1 cartão especial** como primeiro cartão do grupo:

- **Frente**: `📺 FONTE` (para vídeos) ou `🎵 FONTE` (para músicas)
- **Verso**: URL completa ou referência da fonte (artista + nome da música)

**Exemplo — Vídeo do YouTube:**
```
Frente: 📺 FONTE
Verso:  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ
```

**Exemplo — Letra de música:**
```
Frente: 🎵 FONTE
Verso:  Coldplay — Fix You (https://genius.com/Coldplay-fix-you-lyrics)
```

Os cartões de vocabulário que seguem referem-se àquela fonte até o próximo cartão-metadado.

### Formato dos cartões de vocabulário (Component C)

O formato segue o tier do aluno, adaptado ao material autêntico:

| Tier | Formato do cartão Component C |
|------|-------------------------------|
| **Tier 1** | Frente: frase da música/vídeo com `[MAIÚSCULO]`; Verso: `palavra = tradução` |
| **Tier 2** | Frente: frase com `[MAIÚSCULO]`; Verso: `(classe) = tradução ☞ colocação` + tradução da frase |
| **Tier 3** | Frente: frase com `[MAIÚSCULO]`; Verso: `(classe) = definição EN ☞ colocações` + tradução da frase |

### Metas do Component C

| Tier | Mínimo de cartões de vocabulário | Mínimo de fontes |
|------|--------------------------------:|----------------:|
| Tier 1 | 5 | 1 |
| Tier 2 | 7 | 1 |
| Tier 3 | 8 | 2 |

> Tier 3 exige 2 fontes diferentes para incentivar diversificação de registros.

### Verificação programática de fonte

O script de avaliação buscará cartões cuja frente contenha `FONTE` (case-insensitive) no baralho Component C. A partir deles:

1. Extrai URL ou referência do verso
2. Valida formato (URL válida ou padrão `Artista — Título`)
3. Conta cartões de vocabulário associados a cada fonte
4. Gera flags:
   - `SEM_FONTE`: nenhum cartão-metadado encontrado
   - `FONTE_INVALIDA`: verso do cartão-metadado não contém URL nem referência reconhecível
   - `POUCOS_CARTOES`: menos de 5 cartões de vocabulário para uma fonte

## 8. Critérios de avaliação

Fórmula mantida de E02–E04:

```
Nota = 0.25×V + 0.25×C + 0.30×Q + 0.20×E
```

| Componente | Variáveis |
|------------|-----------|
| **V — Volume** | revisões totais / cards criados |
| **C — Consistência** | dias de estudo / distribuição temporal |
| **Q — Qualidade** | retenção (70%) + maturidade (30%) |
| **E — Engajamento** | tempo de resposta + distribuição dos botões Again/Hard/Good/Easy |

### Penalidades

| Penalidade | Condição | Efeito |
|------------|----------|--------|
| **RET100_CAP** | Retenção = 100% (≥ 30 revisões) + maturidade < 10% | Nota final limitada a 40 |
| **SEM_FONTE** | Component C sem cartão-metadado de fonte | Desconto de 10 pontos na nota do Component C |

> **Maturidade** (ivl ≥ 21d): com 19 dias de E05, a janela é mais curta do que E04. Alunos que criarem cartões nos primeiros dias e revisarem consistentemente ainda poderão ter cartões próximos da maturidade ao final.
